1Do you actually need a new study permit?

This is the question worth getting right. The short answer: yes, you do — but for a different reason than you might think.

The rule for program changes at the same school

IRCC's policy is clear: changing programs at the same DLI does NOT require a new study permit — you just continue on your existing one.IRCCSFU ISS The November 2024 regulations tightened DLI-to-DLI transfer rules but did not affect same-institution transfers.CIC News May 2025

Why you still need a new one

Three concrete reasons:

  • Your current permit expires August 2026. The M.Sc.A. is 16 months. Your existing permit can't cover it — you need new authorization regardless of the program change.
  • You're outside Canada. The "no new permit needed" rule applies when you're inside Canada and continuing studies. When re-entering Canada under a new program, McGill ISS explicitly recommends having a new permit aligned to that program.McGill ISS A CBSA officer at port of entry seeing a permit for "M.Sc. Physiology" but a new admission letter for "M.Sc.A. Biotechnology" creates avoidable friction.
  • You're getting a new CAQ anyway. Quebec requires a new CAQ for program changes (even master's-to-master's) at McGill.McGill CAQ rules Your federal permit duration should match your CAQ — so a fresh permit aligned to the new program is the clean path.
Bottom line: Apply for a new study permit from outside Canada. This is the path McGill ISS recommends and the one that gives you the cleanest entry at the port of entry. It's not a bureaucratic over-step — it's the right move.

3When to withdraw from the MSc (the order matters)

You felt you need to withdraw. The short answer: yes, eventually — but not yet. Timing matters.

For IRCC: not a precondition

Whether you've formally withdrawn from the MSc is not what IRCC looks at. IRCC's compliance concern is whether McGill reports you as compliant (enrolled / on authorized leave). The withdrawal itself is a McGill administrative step.

For McGill: yes, you'll need to — but later

McGill won't let you be registered in two graduate programs simultaneously. The M.Sc.A. registration will require the M.Sc. to be closed out.

The right order of operations

  1. Confirm M.Sc.A. acceptance fully (deposit paid, GPS confirms the spot is locked).
  2. Get the new CAQ approved.
  3. Submit and receive the new study permit.
  4. THEN formally withdraw from the M.Sc. — typically the same time as activating M.Sc.A. registration in Minerva.
Why not withdraw first? If McGill currently has you on authorized leave from the M.Sc., that's a "compliant" status in DLI reports. A formal withdrawal before the new permit is approved could change your status from "on authorized leave" → "no longer enrolled," which is a worse signal in any compliance report that gets generated between now and your new permit approval. Hold the line; switch over once the new permit is in hand.

When you email McGill GPS (Step 2 item #1), confirm this order with them. They handle these transitions routinely. ♡

What NOT to put in the LoE
  • The PI's name or any identifying detail about them
  • Emotional words: "devastating," "nightmare," "no choice," "trauma," "toxic"
  • Any claim that contradicts your transcript (verify first!)
  • Future immigration intent ("I hope to work in Canada after…")
  • Generic praise of Canada or McGill
  • Long apologetic openers
  • Speculation about why the PI behaved the way they did
